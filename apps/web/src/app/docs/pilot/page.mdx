# The Pilot

The Pilot runs your job search without you. You tell it what you're looking
for and set some limits. It then finds and scores jobs, applies, contacts
people and follows up, reads recruiter replies, and drafts interview replies,
prep notes, and posts.

You can see everything it does in a journal that updates live. When it needs
a decision from you, it asks a short question, and it can send that question
to your phone.

The Pilot does the same work as [campaigns](/docs/campaigns-and-skills), so
you don't need to run those yourself. They're all still there if you want to
do something by hand.

## How it works

The Pilot does its work in small steps called **cycles**. Each cycle does one
thing:

```text
looks for work ──► picks the most useful task ─┬─► finds jobs ────┐
                                               ├─► scores them ───┤
                                               ├─► applies ───────┼─► writes to the journal
                                               ├─► reaches out ───┤
                                               └─► writing tasks ─┘
```

1. **Look for work.** A small program on your computer asks the server what
   needs doing: questions you've answered, jobs you've approved, replies to
   read, searches that are due. If there's nothing, it goes back to sleep
   without starting the AI, so an idle Pilot doesn't use any of your Claude
   or Codex quota.
2. **Pick a task.** If there is work, it takes the most important task and
   starts the AI on it with a fresh, empty context.
3. **Do the work.** Browser tasks go to a helper agent built for that one
   job. One finds new jobs on a board, one scores them, one fills in a single
   application, and one finds a contact and writes the message. Each helper
   only reads what it needs, which keeps usage down. Tasks that are just
   writing, like reading a reply or making prep notes, the AI does itself.
4. **Write it down.** The result goes into the journal. If more work is
   waiting, the next cycle starts a few seconds later. If not, the Pilot
   waits for your check interval.

It does one task per cycle on purpose. Each result is saved on the server
before the next task starts, so if your computer crashes, restarts, or goes to
sleep, nothing is lost and the next cycle carries on from there. The program
also restarts runs that get stuck and starts up again after a reboot, so you
don't have to keep a browser tab open.

## Starting it

1. Check that the agent is running in the dock on the right of the
   dashboard. If it's offline, see [Getting started](/docs/getting-started).
2. Open **Pilot** in the workspace, write your instructions, and save.
3. Press **Start**.

The first start links your computer to the Pilot. From then on the terminal
host keeps it running after a reboot, crash, or sleep until you stop it.

## Your instructions

The Instructions tab shows **Goals** and **Searches** at the top. Everything
else is optional and sits under **Advanced settings**.

- **Goals.** Describe what you want in your own words: the kind of role,
  what matters most, and any hard limits. For example, "senior TypeScript,
  remote, at least $150k, by October". This is the only part you have to fill
  in.
- **Searches.** The Pilot writes these from your goals. A search that keeps
  finding new jobs runs again soon; one that keeps coming up empty runs less
  often. You can see each search here, with how many jobs it found recently
  and when it runs next, but you can't edit them.
- **Operating limits.** How many applications a day, the lowest match score
  worth applying to, and how often to check for work.
- **Networking.** Off until you turn it on. Set a daily limit and how long to
  wait before following up, then choose how much freedom it has on each
  channel. Email can be **Draft only**, **Review each**, or **Auto-send**.
  LinkedIn goes no further than **Review each**, so nothing is ever sent
  there without you.
- **Boards.** Which job boards to search, in order. The Pilot moves to the
  next board each cycle so they all get searched. If you leave this empty,
  each search sticks to the board it was created on.
- **Platforms.** Where the Pilot may draft posts about you. It never
  publishes them; you review every one.

The goals guide the Pilot's judgment, but the limits are hard rules. The
server enforces them, so the Pilot can't go over a limit however your goals
are worded. You can change anything at any time, and the next cycle picks up
the change.

## What it does alone and what it asks about

| Does on its own | Always asks you first |
| --- | --- |
| Applies to good matches, up to your daily limit | Login codes it can't read itself, from SMS or an authenticator app |
| Reads sign-in codes that arrive in your connected email | Salary and screening questions on an application |
| Sends networking emails you set to Auto-send, up to your limit | Sending a LinkedIn message |
| Follows up on networking messages, as your settings allow | Publishing any post |
| Reads recruiter replies and suggests a new status | Sending an interview reply it drafted |
| Drafts emails, follow-ups, interview replies, prep notes, and posts | Whether to restart a campaign you paused |

Everything in the right-hand column turns into a question for you. The Pilot
never sends, posts, or changes an application's status without your say.

## Questions and notifications

When the Pilot needs your decision, it creates a **question**: a small card
under **Needs attention** on the Overview tab. Anything that depends on the
answer waits until you reply. To get a push notification when a question
comes in, even with the dashboard closed, turn on notifications under
**Settings → Notifications**. You do this once per device.

You don't have to answer. **Skip question** closes the card and sends a parked job
back to the queue, so the Pilot retries it without your answer - useful once
you've fixed the cause yourself, such as saving a missing login. **Skip
application** records the job as skipped by you, logs it in the activity feed,
and closes every other open question about that job; the Pilot won't apply to
it again.

Most questions wait as long as you need. Login codes are different. They
expire after about five minutes, and if you miss one the Pilot skips that job
and moves on. Codes sent by email never reach you as questions, because the
agent reads them from your inbox.

## The journal and daily summary

Everything the Pilot does is logged in the **journal** on the Activity tab.
You can filter it by type, or group it **by cycle** to see what each cycle
did, how it went, and how long it took. The Overview tab shows the latest
entries and when the next cycle is due.

Once a day, after 07:00 UTC, the Pilot writes a summary of the past 24 hours:
applications sent, replies read, messages sent, and questions waiting for
you. **Export** downloads the whole history as a file.

## Interviews

When a recruiter invites you to interview, the Pilot drafts a short reply with
your availability and shows it to you for approval. It never sends that reply
itself. It also writes prep notes covering behavioral, technical, and system
design questions plus notes on the company, and attaches them to the
application.

## Troubleshooting

- **The host is offline.** Start the agent from the dock: one click on
  Windows, or one of the commands it shows on macOS and Linux. The Pilot picks
  up again by itself once the host is back.
- **The Pilot paused after several stuck runs.** The host stops to avoid
  wasting your machine's time. Look at the terminal in the dock to see what
  got stuck, then start the Pilot again.
- **One board keeps failing.** The Pilot checks that board once and writes
  what it found in the journal. Usually it's an expired login, and it signs
  back in on the spot. Other boards keep running either way.
