# The Pilot

The Pilot is JobPilot's autonomous mode. Write your **instructions** once, the
goals and the limits, and the local agent runs your search on its own. It finds
and scores roles, applies to them, reaches out to contacts and follows up, reads
recruiter replies, and drafts interview replies, prep sheets, and posts.

Everything it does lands in a live journal. The few decisions only you can make
arrive as one-tap questions, on your phone if you enable notifications.

It runs the [campaign modes](/docs/campaigns-and-skills) for you: searching,
applying, and networking all happen inside its cycles. Every dashboard action
stays available whenever you want to do something by hand.

## How a cycle works

The Pilot works in short, self-contained **cycles**:

1. **Sense.** The server compiles a ranked agenda of everything worth doing
   right now: your answers to act on, approved jobs, replies to review,
   searches that are due.
2. **Act.** The agent takes the top item and does exactly that one thing:
   one application, one inbox review, one search.
3. **Record.** The outcome is journaled and the agent sleeps, for seconds when
   work is queued or up to your check interval when idle.

One item per cycle is deliberate. Every result is saved to the server before
the next thing starts, so a crash, reboot, or closed lid never loses work; the
next cycle picks up exactly where the last one stopped. A small orchestrator on
your machine drives the loop, restarts stuck runs, and starts cycles on its own
after a reboot, so no browser tab needs to stay open.

## Starting it

1. Make sure your local agent terminal is running (the dock down the right side
   of the dashboard). See [Getting started](/docs/getting-started) if it's
   offline.
2. Open **Pilot** in the workspace, write your instructions, and save.
3. Click **Start**.

Starting pairs your machine with the Pilot once; after that the host runs
cycles on boot, crash, or wake until you stop it.

## Your instructions

**Goals** and **Searches** sit at the top of the Instructions tab. The rest is
optional tuning, folded behind **Advanced settings**.

- **Goals.** Plain-language direction: roles, priorities, constraints
  ("senior TypeScript remote, at least $150k, by October"). This is the only
  part you have to write.
- **Searches.** The Pilot creates these from your goals and re-runs them on
  demand, soon after a run that turns up new roles and less often once a
  search keeps coming up dry. The Instructions page shows them read-only, with
  each one's recent yield and next check.
- **Operating limits.** Daily apply cap, minimum match score, check interval.
- **Networking.** Off by default. You set a daily cap and a follow-up delay,
  then an autonomy level per channel: email can be **draft only**, **review
  each**, or **auto-send**; LinkedIn stops at **review each**, because nothing
  is ever sent there without you.
- **Boards.** Which job boards the Pilot searches, and in what order. It moves
  to the next board each cycle, so all of them get worked. Leave it empty and
  each saved search stays on the board it was created with.
- **Platforms.** Where the Pilot drafts self-promotion posts. Posting is
  always review-only.

Your goals text handles the judgment calls. The caps are a different thing: the
server enforces those, so the Pilot can't exceed one no matter how the goals are
worded. Edit any of it any time, and changes take effect on the next cycle.

## What runs on its own and what asks you

| Runs on its own | Always asks you |
| --- | --- |
| Applies to matching roles within your daily cap | A login code it can't read, from SMS or an authenticator app |
| Reads emailed sign-in codes from your connected mailbox | Salary and screening questions on an application |
| Sends networking emails you set to auto-send, within your cap | Sending a LinkedIn message |
| Follows up on networking messages per your autonomy setting | Posting any self-promotion publicly |
| Reviews recruiter replies in your inbox (proposes moves) | Sending a drafted interview reply |
| Drafts emails, follow-ups, interview replies, prep sheets, and posts | Whether to resume a campaign you paused |

Anything in the right column surfaces as a question. Nothing sends, posts, or
changes an application's status without you.

## Questions and push notifications

When the Pilot hits a decision only you can make, it posts a **question**, a
one-decision card under **Needs attention** on the Overview tab, and parks the
related work until you answer. Turn on **Notifications** under
**Settings → Notifications** (per device) to get a web push when a question
opens, even with the tab closed.

You don't have to answer. **Skip question** closes the card and sends a parked job
back to the queue, so the Pilot retries it without your answer - useful once
you've fixed the cause yourself, such as saving a missing login. **Skip
application** records the job as skipped by you, logs it in the activity feed,
and closes every other open question about that job; the Pilot won't apply to
it again.

Most questions wait as long as you need. Login codes are the exception: they
expire after about five minutes, and if you miss one the Pilot skips that job
cleanly and moves on. Codes that arrive by email never become a question at
all, since the agent reads them out of your mailbox itself.

## The journal and daily digest

Every action lands in the **journal** on the Activity tab, a live feed you can
filter by kind or group **by cycle** to see what each cycle did, its outcome,
and how long it took. The Overview tab shows the latest activity plus when the
next cycle will wake. Once a day, after 07:00 UTC, the last 24 hours roll into
a **digest**: applications, replies reviewed, messages sent, questions waiting.
**Export** downloads the full history as a file.

## Interview autonomy

When a recruiter's reply is an interview invite, the Pilot drafts a short
availability reply and posts it as an approval card; it never sends on its
own. It also builds a **prep sheet** (behavioral, technical, system design,
company notes) and attaches it to the application.

## Troubleshooting

- **Host offline.** Start the agent again from the dock: one click on Windows,
  or one of the commands it offers on macOS and Linux. The Pilot resumes on its
  own once the host is back.
- **Pilot paused after repeated stuck runs.** The host takes a break to
  protect your machine. Check the dock terminal for what got stuck, then
  start it again.
- **A board keeps failing.** The Pilot probes it once and journals what it
  found, usually an expired login it re-establishes on the spot. Everything
  else keeps running either way.
