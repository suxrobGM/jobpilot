# The Pilot

The Pilot runs your job search for you. You tell it what you're looking for
and set a few rules. It then finds and scores jobs, applies, contacts people
and follows up, reads recruiter replies, and drafts interview replies, prep
notes, and posts.

It works through the agent on your own computer, in your own Claude Code or
Codex session, so you can watch every step. When it needs a decision, it asks,
and it can send that question to your phone.

## The Pilot page

**Pilot** is the first page in the sidebar. It reads like a conversation with
an assistant who reports back as it works.

- **The header** names the Pilot and shows whether it's up, how many
  applications it sent today against your daily limit, and the **Autopilot**
  switch.
- **The thread** fills the middle, newest at the bottom, split by day. The
  Pilot speaks in plain text; your messages sit in bubbles. Work it did close
  together reads as one message ("Applied to Acme (82) and Globex (74).") with
  the results underneath: a line per job it applied to or task it ran, linked
  to the job, and one line totalling its searches ("Searched 5 times · 97 new
  jobs · 5 of 27 scored cleared your bar · best Acme (80)"). Your messages, its
  replies, changes with **Undo**, and the morning summary appear in order.
- **At the bottom of the thread**, below the newest item, the Pilot says what
  still needs doing: any setup step it's missing, notices worth a look, and
  everything waiting on you, answered right there. The last line is what it's
  doing now, with the buttons for it: **Start agent** while the agent is
  offline, or **Watch**, **Tell this task** and **Stop** while a task runs.
- **The message box** under the thread is how you talk to it. While it's
  empty, a few suggested prompts sit above it.
- **The side column** shows **This week** (applications sent, interviews, and a
  bar per day), your **Searches** with how many applications each sent this
  week (paused ones fold into one line with **Show** and **Resume all**), and
  your **Rules**. On a phone these sit behind a button above the thread.

## Autopilot

With **autopilot on**, the Pilot picks its own work: it runs your searches,
scores what they find, applies to good matches, follows up, and keeps your
inbox read. With **autopilot off**, it does only what you ask for: your
messages, buttons you press, and questions you answer.

Pause it whenever you like, from any device. Anything you ask for still runs.

## Talking to it

Type in the message box like you would to a person: "Stop applying to jobs
that need a security clearance", "Find research assistant roles at
universities in Pennsylvania", or "Why did you skip the Waymo job?". The
Pilot answers in the thread, ahead of any other work. If you ask it to change
something, it makes the change and shows it to you with **Undo**.

- **Paste a job link** and a chip appears: **Add to your pipeline**. That adds
  the job straight away, without waiting for the Pilot.
- **Tell this task** (on the last line of the thread, while something is running) types
  your words into the task in progress, for example "use my other resume for
  this one". It doesn't stop the task.
- **Stop** stops the task in progress. The Pilot moves on to the next
  one.

Buttons around the app, like **Apply** on a job, **Sync** in Upwork, or
**Rewrite** on outreach drafts, ask the Pilot for that work the same way. The
request shows in the thread and runs next, whether autopilot is on or off.

## Goals and rules

Open **Goals & rules** from the Pilot page.

- **Goals.** What you want, in your own words: the kind of role, what matters
  most, and any hard limits. For example, "senior TypeScript, remote, at
  least $150k, by October". This is the only part you have to fill in.
- **Rules.** How many applications a day, the lowest match score worth
  applying to, and **deal-breakers**: things that rule a job out, like
  "security clearance" or "unpaid". The server enforces the rules, so the
  Pilot can't go past them however your goals are worded.
- **Searches.** The Pilot writes these from your goals and gives each a plain
  name. A search that keeps finding new jobs runs again soon; one that keeps
  coming up empty runs less often. You can pause, resume, or delete any of
  them, or ask the Pilot in a message to add one.
- **Standing answers.** When an application asks something that fits other
  jobs too, like your salary expectation, you can save your answer and the
  Pilot uses it on every application.

Every edit, yours or the Pilot's, is saved as a **change** in the thread with
**Undo** for 7 days. The Pilot changes its own searches freely, but it only
*suggests* changes to your goals, rules, and standing answers. You apply or
dismiss each suggestion.

## Pilot settings

**Settings → Behavior** holds the rest. The defaults work for most people.

- **Networking.** Off until you turn it on. Set a daily limit and how long to
  wait before following up, then choose how much freedom it has on each
  channel. Email can be **Draft only**, **Review each**, or **Auto-send**.
  LinkedIn goes no further than **Review each**, so nothing is ever sent
  there without you.
- **Research** and **Job alerts.** See below.
- **Check interval.** How often an idle Pilot looks for new work.

Which boards to search, in what order, and each board's login are on the
**Job boards** page. Add or remove boards there too.

### Research

Boards miss many postings, mostly university and research-institute portals
and lab pages. Research searches the open web for postings that fit your goals,
reads each one, scores it, and adds it to your pipeline. From there good
matches are applied to like any other.

- **When it runs.** Once when you first set goals, then weekly, and within a
  day when most of your searches keep coming up empty. **Research now** runs it
  next.
- **Cap-exempt employers.** Universities, their affiliated hospitals and
  institutes, and nonprofit or government research employers can sponsor an
  H-1B outside the yearly cap. When the Pilot finds evidence of that, the job
  gets a **Cap-exempt** tag that links to it. Turn on **Prefer H-1B
  cap-exempt employers** in your rules to have those jobs applied to first.
- **Boards worth adding.** The run summary names sites that kept turning up
  good postings. The Pilot never adds a board itself.
- It never logs in, signs up, contacts anyone, or applies while researching.

### Job alerts

If you get job alert emails from boards like LinkedIn or Indeed, the Pilot
can read them on a schedule and add the jobs to your pipeline. **Run now**
imports them on the next run.

## Notices

Near the bottom of the thread, the Pilot points out patterns in your own data,
each with one button to act on it and **Dismiss**:

- your email isn't connected, or needs reconnecting;
- mail the Pilot hasn't read for more than two days;
- 25 or more applications in three weeks with no replies, so you can ask it
  why;
- lots of jobs scoring just under your minimum score, with a button to lower
  it (you can undo that);
- autopilot running for a week while using under a quarter of your daily
  limit, so you can ask it to find more jobs.

**Dismiss** hides a notice for two weeks.

## The pipeline

**Pipeline** lists every job the Pilot found, by stage: **Found**, **To
apply**, **Applied**, **Needs you**, **Skipped**, and **Failed**. Found lists
the best matches first; a job still waiting for its score says **Scoring…**.
Filter by feed (each search, job alerts, research, links you added, or your
own runs), board, or text, and open a job to read its brief and score. Tick
several jobs to **Approve** or **Skip** them at once, or **Retry** failed ones.
Jobs you already have are never added twice.

## Watching it work

The agent button at the bottom of the sidebar, or **Watch** on the last line
of the thread, opens the **agent drawer**:

- **Steps** shows the task in progress as short, readable lines: "Opened
  boards.greenhouse.io/acme/jobs/42", "Filled 6 fields", "Clicked Submit
  application". It works from any device, including your phone. Steps never
  include what was typed into a form or the text of a page.
- **Terminal** is the live agent session itself. It only works on the
  computer running the agent.

You can type in the terminal too. When you do, the Pilot holds off starting
new tasks for 3 minutes so it doesn't interrupt you. **Hand back** on the
last line of the thread ends that wait early.

## How it works

The Pilot does its work as a series of small **tasks**:

```text
looks for work ──► picks the most useful task ─┬─► finds jobs ────┐
                                               ├─► scores them ───┤
                                               ├─► applies ───────┼─► reports in the thread
                                               ├─► reaches out ───┤
                                               └─► writing tasks ─┘
```

1. **Look for work.** A small program on your computer, the agent, asks the
   server what needs doing: your messages and requests, questions you've
   answered, jobs to apply to, replies to read, searches that are due. If
   there's nothing, it goes back to sleep without starting the AI, so an idle
   Pilot doesn't use any of your Claude or Codex quota.
2. **Pick a task.** Your messages come first, then what you asked for, then
   everything else by importance. It starts the AI on the task with a fresh,
   empty context.
3. **Do the work.** Browser tasks go to a helper built for that one job: one
   finds new jobs on a board, one scores them, one fills in a single
   application, and one finds a contact and writes the message. Each helper
   only reads what it needs, which keeps usage down.
4. **Report.** The result goes into the thread, and the next task starts.

Each result is saved on the server before the next task starts, so if your
computer crashes, restarts, or goes to sleep, nothing is lost. The agent
connects to the Pilot automatically once you open JobPilot with the agent
running, restarts tasks that get stuck, and starts again after a reboot, so
you don't need to keep a browser tab open.

## What it does alone and what it asks about

| Does on its own | Always asks you first |
| --- | --- |
| Applies to good matches, up to your daily limit | Login codes it can't read itself, from SMS or an authenticator app |
| Reads sign-in codes that arrive in your connected email | Salary and screening questions it has no standing answer for |
| Sends networking emails you set to Auto-send, up to your limit | Sending a LinkedIn message |
| Follows up on networking messages, as your settings allow | Publishing any post |
| Reads recruiter replies and suggests a new status | Sending an interview reply it drafted |
| Drafts emails, follow-ups, interview replies, prep notes, and posts | Changing your goals, rules, or standing answers |
| Adds, renames, and pauses its own searches | Adding a board it suggests |

Everything in the right-hand column waits at the bottom of the thread until
you decide. The Pilot never sends, posts, or changes an application's status
without your say.

## Questions and notifications

When the Pilot needs your decision, it asks a **question** at the bottom of
the thread, where you answer it. Anything that depends on the answer
waits until you reply. To get a push notification when a question comes in,
even with JobPilot closed, turn on notifications under **Settings →
Notifications**. You do this once per device.

Most questions wait as long as you need. Login codes are different. They
expire after about five minutes, and if you miss one the Pilot skips that job
and moves on. Codes sent by email never reach you as questions, because the
agent reads them from your inbox.

## The morning summary

Once a day, after 07:00 UTC, the Pilot posts a summary of the past 24 hours
in the thread: applications sent, replies read, messages sent, and questions
waiting for you. A day with nothing to report shows as one quiet line. **Settings → Diagnostics** has the full history of runs, what
each one cost in tokens, and an export.

## Interviews

When a recruiter invites you to interview, the Pilot drafts a short reply with
your availability and shows it to you for approval. It never sends that reply
itself. It also writes prep notes covering behavioral, technical, and system
design questions plus notes on the company, and attaches them to the
application.

## Troubleshooting

- **The agent is offline.** Start it from the agent drawer: one click on
  Windows, or one of the commands it shows on macOS and Linux. The Pilot picks
  up again by itself once the agent is back.
- **It isn't starting anything.** Read the last line of the thread. "Autopilot
  is off" means it only runs what you ask for. "You're using the terminal"
  clears 3 minutes after you stop typing, or at once with **Hand back**.
- **A task keeps getting stuck.** Open **Steps** or **Terminal** in the agent
  drawer to see where, then **Stop** it or tell it what to do with
  **Tell this task**.
- **One board keeps failing.** The Pilot checks that board once and reports
  what it found. Usually it's an expired login, and it signs back in on the
  spot. Other boards keep running either way.
