# The Pilot

The Pilot runs your job search. You tell it what jobs you want. It finds
and scores jobs, applies, reaches out to people, reads replies, and drafts
interview replies and prep notes. When it needs a decision, it asks.

It works through the agent on your computer, so you can watch every step.

## The Pilot page

- **Top bar.** Applications sent today against your daily limit, and the
  **Autopilot** switch.
- **Needs you** (left). Everything waiting on you: questions to answer,
  suggested changes to approve, applications that stopped for your input, and
  notices. You answer most of them right there. A stopped application opens
  on its own page.
- **The thread** (middle). The Pilot reports what it did, newest at the
  bottom: "Applied to Acme (82) and Globex (74)", with a line per job below.
  Your messages, changes with **Undo**, and the morning summary show here too.
  The last line says what it's doing now.
- **Overview** (right). A chart of the week, where the Pilot looks (the
  boards it rotates, your searches with applications sent from each this
  week, job alert emails, and web search), and your rules.

On a phone, these are three tabs: **Chat**, **Needs you**, and **Overview**.

## Getting started

Tell the Pilot what you want in the message box, the way you'd brief a
recruiter: "Senior frontend roles, remote in the US, at least $150k. Skip
crypto and agencies." It turns that into a few searches and rules and shows
the change in the thread with **Undo**. Turn on **Autopilot** and it starts.

If you turn on autopilot first, the Pilot sets up searches from your resume.
Tell it later if you want something different.

## Autopilot

With autopilot **on**, the Pilot picks its own work: runs searches, scores
results, applies to good matches, follows up, and reads your inbox.

With autopilot **off**, it only does what you ask: your messages, buttons you
press, and questions you answer.

## Talking to it

Type in the message box as you would to a person:

- "Stop applying to jobs that need a security clearance"
- "Find research assistant roles at universities in Pennsylvania"
- "Why did you skip the Waymo job?"

Your messages go ahead of other work. When you ask for a change, it makes it
and shows it in the thread with **Undo**.

Paste job links, or press **Job links**, to add jobs to your pipeline at
once. To search beyond your boards now, ask: "Find more jobs beyond my boards",
or narrow it, like "find ML roles at university labs in PA". The Pilot says what
it will search before it starts, and **Cancel** on that line in the thread stops
it while it's still queued. To change your limits, choose **Edit** next to
**Rules** in the Overview.

Buttons elsewhere in the app, like **Apply now** on a job, work the same way:
the request shows in the thread and runs next, with autopilot on or off.

While a task runs, its line in the thread has three buttons:

- **Watch** opens the task's steps.
- **Tell this task** sends a note to the task in progress, like "use my other
  resume for this one", without stopping it.
- **Stop** ends the task. The Pilot moves on.

## Searches and rules

To change what the Pilot looks for, tell it in the message box: "add
research engineer roles", "only Boston and remote", "drop the data analyst
search". To see everything at once, choose **Manage** next to **Sources** in
the Overview. That page lists your searches, rules, and standing answers.

- **Searches.** What the Pilot looks for. Each shows what it's after in your
  words, like "Series A-C startups; needs OPT sponsorship". A search that
  keeps finding new jobs runs more often; one that comes up empty runs less.
  **Resume all** under **Searches** restarts paused ones.
- **Rules.** Applications per day, the lowest match score worth applying to,
  and **deal-breakers** that rule a job out, like "security clearance" or
  "unpaid". The server enforces these, so the Pilot can't go past them.
- **Standing answers.** When a form asks something that applies to other jobs
  too, like your salary expectation, save your answer and the Pilot reuses it.

What you ask for in a message is done right away. On its own, the Pilot
changes only its searches; changes to your rules and standing answers are
suggested, and you approve or dismiss each one under **Needs you**. Every
change can be undone for 7 days.

## Sources

The Overview's **Sources** section lists where the Pilot finds jobs.
Job alert emails have a button that imports them now; for a web search now,
ask the Pilot to find more jobs.

- **Job boards.** The Pilot runs each search on the boards you check, one board
  per cycle, in the order you set. A search pinned to a board stays on that
  board: open it under **Searches & rules** and choose its **Board**. With no board checked, unpinned searches can't run, and the Pilot asks
  you to pick one. Set boards, their order, and their logins on the
  **Job boards** page.
- **Job alert emails.** Reads job alert emails from boards like LinkedIn and
  adds the jobs. Set the schedule under **Settings → Behavior**.
- **Web search.** Searches the web for postings your boards miss, such as
  company career pages and university and lab portals. It runs once your first searches exist, then
  weekly, and sooner when your searches keep coming up empty. It never logs in,
  signs up, or applies while researching.

## Settings

**Settings → Behavior**. The defaults suit most people.

- **Networking.** Off until you turn it on. Set a daily limit and how long to
  wait before following up. Email can be **Draft only**, **Review each**, or
  **Auto-send**. LinkedIn goes no further than **Review each**.
- **Job alerts.** Whether alert emails import on a schedule, and when.
- **Check interval.** How often an idle Pilot looks for work.

### Cap-exempt employers

Universities, their hospitals and institutes, and nonprofit or government
research employers can sponsor an H-1B outside the yearly cap. When the Pilot
finds evidence of this, the job gets a **Cap-exempt** tag. Turn on **Prefer
H-1B cap-exempt employers** in your rules to apply to those first.

## What it does alone and what it asks about

| Does on its own | Asks you first |
| --- | --- |
| Applies to good matches, up to your daily limit | Login codes from SMS or an authenticator app |
| Reads sign-in codes sent to your connected email | Screening questions it has no saved answer for |
| Sends networking emails set to **Auto-send** | Sending a LinkedIn message |
| Follows up, as your settings allow | Sending an interview reply |
| Reads replies and suggests a new status | Changing your rules or standing answers |
| Drafts emails, interview replies, and prep notes | Publishing a post |
| Adds, renames, and pauses its own searches | Adding a board it found |

## Questions and notifications

Questions wait under **Needs you** for as long as you need. Login codes are
the exception: they expire in about five minutes, and if you miss one, the
Pilot skips that job.

To get questions on your phone, even with JobPilot closed, turn on
**Settings → Notifications** on each device.

## Notices

**Needs you** also points out patterns in your data, each with a button to act
on it:

- your email isn't connected, or needs reconnecting;
- mail unread for more than two days;
- 25 or more applications in three weeks with no replies;
- many jobs scoring just under your minimum score;
- a week of autopilot using under a quarter of your daily limit.

**Dismiss** hides a notice for two weeks.

## The pipeline

**Pipeline** holds every job the Pilot found, in four tabs:

- **To review.** Best matches first, with each job's score against your
  minimum. **Approve** or **Skip** one, or select several.
- **In progress.** Approved, applying, or waiting on you.
- **Applied.** Every application, from applied to offer. See
  [application statuses](/docs/skills).
- **Not applied.** Skipped or failed, with the reason. **Retry** a failed
  job, or **Rescan** a skipped one.

Filter by source (a search, job alerts, research, links you added), board, or
text. Jobs found over a month ago show a clock, and a banner offers to skip
them all. A job is never added twice.

## Watching it work

The agent drawer, opened from the agent button on the left rail or **Watch**
in the thread, has two tabs:

- **Steps.** The task in progress as short lines: "Opened
  boards.greenhouse.io/acme/jobs/42", "Filled 6 fields". Works on any device,
  including your phone. Steps never show what was typed into forms.
- **Terminal.** The live agent session. Only on the computer running the
  agent.

If you type in the terminal, the Pilot holds new tasks for 3 minutes so it
doesn't interrupt you. **Hand back** in the thread ends the wait early.

## How it works

The Pilot does its work as small tasks, one at a time:

1. The agent asks the server what needs doing: your messages, buttons you
   pressed, answered questions, jobs to apply to, replies to read, searches
   that are due. With nothing to do, the AI stays off and uses none of your
   quota.
2. It takes the most important task. Your messages come first, then your
   requests, then the rest.
3. It does the task with a fresh context. Browser work goes to a helper made
   for one thing: finding jobs on a board, scoring them, filling in one
   application, or writing to one contact.
4. It reports the result in the thread and moves on.

Results are saved as it goes, so a crash or sleep loses nothing. After a
restart, start the agent again and it picks up where it stopped. You don't
need to keep JobPilot open in a browser.

Each morning after 07:00 UTC, the thread gets a summary of the last day:
applications sent, replies read, messages sent, and questions waiting.
**Settings → Diagnostics** has every run, its token cost, and an export.

## Interviews

When a recruiter invites you to interview, the Pilot drafts a reply with your
availability and waits for your approval. It also writes prep notes covering
behavioral, technical, and system design questions, plus notes on the company,
and attaches them to the application.

## Troubleshooting

- **The agent is offline.** Open the agent drawer and start it. The Pilot
  carries on once it's back.
- **Nothing is happening.** Read the last line of the thread. "Autopilot is
  off" means it only does what you ask. "You're using the terminal" clears 3
  minutes after you stop typing, or at once with **Hand back**.
- **A task is stuck.** Open **Steps** or **Terminal** to see where, then
  **Stop** it or use **Tell this task**.
- **One board keeps failing.** Usually an expired login. The Pilot checks the
  board, signs back in if it can, and reports what it found. Other boards keep
  running.
